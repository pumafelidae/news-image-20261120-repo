LEAD · BRUSSELS · EUROPE ◉1

Britain and EU Agree Reset Deal in Brussels

Downing Street said Britain and the EU have agreed a reset deal, concluding talks Friday: a youth mobility scheme of 100,000 visas a year each way, for under-31s, up to three years; a food-safety deal ending checks on British food and drink exports; and linked emissions trading systems. The food deal alone is put at up to £5.1bn a year. PM Andy Burnham called it “the single biggest growth intervention in this parliament”. A tuition-fees row still threatens signing; a joint statement follows this afternoon.

LEAD · KYIV · EUROPE ◉2

Russia Hammers Kyiv in Overnight Missile and Drone Barrage

Russia fired at least 100 missiles and about 400 drones — 120 of them jet-powered — at Ukraine overnight into Friday, Kyiv said, in an assault lasting over eight hours. Eight were killed and at least 28 injured; three Kyiv bridges were damaged, halting traffic between its banks, and power was cut in several regions — grid operator Ukrenergo called the network situation “extremely critical”. Homes in Kharkiv and buildings in Dnipropetrovsk, Lviv, Vinnytsia, Sumy and Chernihiv were hit. Moscow called it a “massive strike” on military and energy facilities, Odesa port and Kyiv transport infrastructure.

LEAD · MARKETS · ASIA ◉3

Asia Rout Deepens as Nikkei and Kospi Plunge at the Close

Asian shares sank further Friday after Wall Street’s Thursday rout: South Korea’s KOSPI fell 4.08% to 4,928.51 at the open, breaking below 5,000, and closed down 5.21% at 4,869.96 as a sell-side “sidecar” breaker was triggered; Japan’s Nikkei opened at 58,551.56 (–1.50%) and closed down 5.06% at 56,436.65. Samsung fell 7.01% to 183,100 won. MSCI Asia Pacific fell for a seventh straight session. Thursday: Dow –1,101 (–2.19%) to 49,097.06, below 50,000 for the first time since June; S&P 500 –2.02%, Nasdaq –2.25%.

FORMULA 1 · LAS VEGAS ◉4

Rain Wrecks Vegas Practice; Russell Fastest in FP2

Heavy rain wrecked first practice for the Las Vegas Grand Prix — no meaningful times, Kimi Antonelli reporting aquaplaning everywhere. The session ran 00:30–01:30 UTC Friday. Second practice, also wet, produced times: George Russell fastest at 1:47.272, 0.071s ahead of Pierre Gasly and 0.333s ahead of Charles Leclerc; Max Verstappen only 13th (1:50.141) after a power problem.

CYCLONE · INDIA ◉5

Cyclone Akvan Makes Landfall in Odisha

Severe cyclone Akvan, the third severe storm there in a month, crossed India’s east coast near Gopalpur around 08:30 IST Friday, with winds of 70–80 km/h gusting to 90 km/h. More than 300,000 people were evacuated, 423 rescue teams deployed, 69 trains cancelled and the Odisha Assembly postponed to 23 November. No major damage or deaths were reported by late morning.

CHINA · RATES ◉6

China Holds Lending Rates for 18th Month

China’s central bank held its benchmark lending rates for an 18th straight month on Friday: the one-year loan prime rate stays at 3.00% and the five-year at 3.50%, as expected by 23 of 28 economists in a Reuters poll. The People’s Bank of China is making its overnight reverse repo rate of 1.25% its main policy tool, replacing the seven-day repo.

JAPAN · INFLATION ◉7

Japan Core Inflation 2.2%, Rate Rise in Sight

Core inflation rose 2.2% in October year-on-year, data released Friday morning showed — below the 2.3% forecast but up from 2.1% in September and above the Bank of Japan’s 2% target for a second straight month. The reading backs a rise to 1.5% at the BOJ meeting ending 18 December.

CRICKET · WELLINGTON ◉8

Nicholls 175* as NZ Post 368; India Reply 159/1

Henry Nicholls finished unbeaten on 175 and Glenn Phillips scored 118 as New Zealand reached 368 all out on day two of the first Test against India in Wellington, the pair adding 216 runs. India replied with 159 for 1 — Yashasvi Jaiswal 85, KL Rahul 52 not out — in an opening stand of 130, India’s first in 30 innings.

ANTHROPIC · NEW YORK ◉9

Anthropic Slides 6% in Pre-Market After Record Debut

Anthropic shares fell 6% in pre-market trading on Friday to around $91.24 after closing their first Nasdaq day at $97.17, up 17% from the $83 IPO price. The $81.3bn IPO is the largest ever, valuing the company at about $1.8tn fully diluted — but the debut failed to reassure AI investors as the wider tech sell-off deepened.

MYANMAR · ASIA ◉10

Myanmar Calls ICJ Genocide Ruling ‘Unfair’

Myanmar’s government on Friday called the International Court of Justice’s ruling that it committed genocide against the Rohingya “unfair” and “not unanimous” — the judgment was 9–6. The ruling itself came on Thursday; Bangladesh’s foreign minister said on Friday that Rohingya repatriation “will not happen tomorrow or soon”, targeting a safe corridor within 1–1.5 years.

KAKAO · SEOUL ◉3

Seoul Court Upholds Acquittal of Kakao Founder

The Seoul High Court on Friday upheld the acquittal of Kakao Corp founder Kim Beom-su on charges of manipulating the share price of K-pop agency SM Entertainment. Prosecutors had sought 15 years in prison and a 500 million won fine; the court said the evidence did not prove Kim intended to manipulate prices or conspired to do so.

RUSSIA · MOSCOW ◉11

Moscow Rejects NATO-Style Guarantees for Kyiv

Russia on Friday flatly rejected NATO-style security guarantees for Ukraine, with Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov calling them Kyiv’s “quasi-membership” of the Western alliance and “absolutely unacceptable”. The rebuff answers US envoy Jared Kushner’s claim in Berlin that Moscow was “comfortable” with such guarantees. Ryabkov also confirmed a possible visit by European envoys to Moscow.

POLAND · BORDER ◉12

Poland Probes Unknown Object Near Ukraine Border

Poland’s armed forces on Friday investigated reports of an unknown object falling in south-eastern Poland near the Ukraine border, in the village of Chmielek, about 45 km from the frontier. “We are checking two possible airspace violations,” said operational command spokesman Jacek Goryszewski. Polish jets were scrambled preventively during Russia’s overnight attack on Ukraine; the alert has been lifted. PM Donald Tusk said there is currently no air threat.

KENYA · NAIROBI ◉13

Kenya Courts Clear 103% of Cases; Top Court’s Land Was Grabbed

Chief Justice Martha Koome on Friday launched Kenya’s 12th annual State of the Judiciary report: courts resolved 576,702 cases against 561,580 filed — a 103% clearance rate, the second straight year above 100% — though 341,000 cases (59% of 579,954 pending) remain backlogged. Koome revealed the Supreme Court’s own land had been grabbed by private parties, leaving the top court without a title deed until it was recovered last year.
